New to Claude? Start here.

Why Claude? Claude is an AI helper you talk to in plain words. Instead of facing a blank page, you describe the job, like a letter, a plan, or a page for your website. Claude drafts it, explains its thinking in normal language, and works through it with you. It is patient with beginners, strong at writing, and careful about following instructions. You do not need to know any code to get real work out of it.

Claude or Claude Code? They are not the same thing.

Claude, the chat

The one at claude.ai, in your browser or the phone app. You type, it answers: writing, summaries, ideas, explanations. It cannot touch the files on your computer. This is where everyone should start.

Claude Code

The same helper, working on your computer. It can create and edit real files, build web pages and videos, and run the tools on this page. When a card below says “open Claude Code,” this is the one it means.

Set up, step by step

  1. Make a free account. Go to claude.ai and sign up with your email. It worked if you are signed in and see an empty chat box.
  2. Try the chat first. Ask for something small and real: “Turn these rough notes into a tidy email.” Keep going until it feels normal. It worked if Claude answers in the chat window. That is regular Claude. Nothing is installed, so nothing can break.
  3. Decide whether you need Claude Code. If you only want writing and answers, the chat is enough and you can stop here. If you want pages, files, or videos made for you, which is what the tools below are for, keep going. It worked if you know which of the two you are setting up, and why.
  4. Check your plan covers Claude Code. Claude Code needs a paid Claude plan (Pro or Max), or a developer account with Anthropic, the company that makes Claude. The free chat plan does not include it. It worked if your account settings on claude.ai show a Pro or Max plan.
  5. Install Claude Code. Open the terminal, the text window on your computer where you type commands. On a Mac it is the app called Terminal; on Windows it is PowerShell. Paste the command for your computer, press Enter, and let it finish.
    curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash

    That is the Mac command. On Windows, paste this one into PowerShell instead:

    irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 | iex
    It worked if you type claude --version, press Enter, and a version number prints back.
  6. Start it inside a project folder. Make a new, empty folder for your project. In the terminal, move into that folder, type claude, and press Enter. A browser window opens so you can sign in. It worked if Claude greets you in the terminal and waits for instructions. Everything it makes will land in this folder.
  7. Give it one small first job. Type what you want in plain words: “Build a simple one-page website for my bakery, with our opening hours and a space for a photo. Explain each step as you go.” It worked if new files appear in your folder and Claude tells you how to open the page. That is your first real job done. The tools below all plug into Claude Code the same way.

How to use this page

  1. Pick one tool. Not five. Pick the one that matches a job you already have this week.
  2. Start with the Start Here picks just below. They are the three best starting points in each group.
  3. Follow the steps on its card, in order. Each card says where to paste or click, and what you should see when it works.
  4. Ignore everything else until that one tool works. You can come back for the next one.

Words used on this page

Repo
A folder of project files stored online, usually on a site called GitHub. The name on each card, like obra/superpowers, is that folder's address. Tap it to visit.
Terminal
The text window on your computer where you type commands instead of clicking buttons. On a Mac it is an app called Terminal. On Windows it is PowerShell or Command Prompt.
Command
One line of text you paste into the terminal, or into Claude Code, and run by pressing Enter. Every command here sits in a dark box with a Copy button.
Claude Code
The version of Claude that works on your computer and builds things with you: pages, files, and videos. The chat talks; Claude Code does the work.
Plugin and skill
A plugin is an add-on that gives Claude Code a new ability. A skill is a set of written instructions that teaches Claude to do one job well, the same way every time.

Before you install anything

  • Only install tools you trust. These tools run on your computer, and add-ons for Claude can run code on your machine. That is normal for tools like these. It means you should install only from the official link on each card, never a lookalike site, and read what an add-on says it will do before you add it.
  • Prices change. Prices and activity on this page were checked in October 2026 and will drift. Several tools are free for one person or a small team and charge once you grow, so check the current price before your team depends on one.
  • Free does not always mean free for business. A few tools have their own rules for business use: Remotion (video), n8n (connecting apps), and parts of PostHog, Twenty, and Anthropic's document skills. Each card flags its own case under “Watch out for”.
  • Where we could not check an install route, the card says so instead of guessing. That is why Audacity's card points you to the official download rather than a command.

Meet
Muse.

Muse is a personal assistant you talk to in plain words. On its own, Muse can think, write, plan, and research with you. Connect the apps and services you already use, and Muse can work inside them too: reading your email, checking your calendar, finding your files, drafting replies, booking tables, and keeping track of your money and your business.

Nothing here is connected yet. You connect each service yourself, one at a time, and only the ones you want. Muse asks before it sends a message or spends money for you.

Who Muse is for

Everyday life

You talk, Muse does the legwork. “What is on my calendar this week, and when am I free Thursday?” Muse can sort a stuffed inbox, find a flight and watch it for delays, book the restaurant, build a playlist for the drive, and show you what your sleep and spending looked like last month.

Busy professionals

Less time re-typing your own life into apps. Muse can turn meeting notes into follow-up messages, draft documents from your own files, pull a number out of a spreadsheet, and answer “what did we decide last month?” from the real transcript instead of your memory.

Entrepreneurs and small businesses

The work of a small team, without hiring one. Muse can check which invoices are unpaid, see which ad is working, follow up leads in your customer records, draft client replies in your voice for your approval, and keep the store, the books, and the calendar in one conversation.

How connecting works

A connector is a link between Muse and one app or service you use, like your email or your calendar. Each card below says what the service is, what Muse can do with it, and why people connect it.

  1. Pick one service below that matches a job you do every week. One is enough to start.
  2. Sign in with that service. Muse walks you through it, and you never hand over your password in a chat.
  3. Ask in plain words, the way you would ask a capable assistant sitting next to you.
  4. You stay in charge. Reading and looking things up is the everyday work. Anything that sends, posts, buys, or changes something goes through you first.

Good to know before you connect anything

  • This is a catalogue, not a connection. Listing a service here does not connect it. You connect each service separately, with that service's own sign-in.
  • Connect one at a time, and only what you will use. Every connection gives Muse a way into that account. Start with the one that saves you the most time each week. You can disconnect any service later.
  • Some connectors need no account. A few look things up in public information, like flight status, places, events, and product prices, and work without a sign-in. The cards say so where that is the case.
